# Grove structure and contract ownership

The k140 structural refactor removes continuation-only levels while retaining
all original task identities, routing kinds, terminal/live outcomes and complete
depth-first execution order. It changes no product result or runtime authority.
This is the contract crosswalk for future work, not a session-status file.

## Reading the live tree

The additive chain is root → k12 Settings repair → k36 ordering evaluation →
k96 keepalive owner design. Each retained brief states its distinct subgoal,
joint completion condition and new shared context. Positions encode dependencies;
reviews/integrations and research pairs are flat siblings. No original node
contains a declared Grove pass series. Historical finite experiment budgets,
fixed runtime slots and their stop rules remain binding.

The useful product increment remains complete Settings behavior plus the shared
harness and separate complete ordinary acceptance. A design, schema expansion or
isolated consumer cannot independently supply that accepted behavior. No separate
Grove is created; future implementation is externalised after complete design.
k23's independently scoped archived-link aids remain their existing separate
session, in the original order.

## Ownership of every original brief

aggregate acceptance, authority limits and historical classifications in that
original brief**, including all its listed headings. The named aggregate owner
must reconcile the entire contract before closure; task owners execute its
specific remaining duties. The table is not a claim that a duty passed.
The current briefs promote operative requirements. The exact originals provide
precise inherited detail when a task needs it; historical handoffs do not become
extra mandatory ancestor reads. Explicit accepted scope amendments in the

## Stable references and preserved originals

[The identity map](identity-map.json) records every original brief path, SHA-256,
heading and current owner, and every original leaf path/name/digest and new path.
The `.txt` copies preserve original bytes and original relative-path spelling;
resolve old relative pointers against the recorded original location, not the
archive directory. Repository-root paths in backticks retain their usual meaning.
The original root's relative links are historical text, not new archive links.
Current navigation uses stable handles and the live brief/task pointers.

Removed node handles remain historical identities in completed task text. A live
task's reference to one now means the owner in this table and its exact archived
contract, not a directory that still exists. All existing terminal task bodies
are unchanged. Live tasks retain their original text with a current ownership
supplement. No causal or product ancestor closes through flattening; old k2/k6
residual-symptom closure and k3's qualified resize acceptance are promoted to root.

## Structural verification

The original tree had 31 briefs totalling 5,220 lines and at most 27 ancestor
directories for a leaf. The retained tree has four briefs totalling 352 lines
and at most three ancestor directories; k125 falls from 26 to three. The
comparison used every original leaf, in order: all 110 keys, handles, kinds and
outcomes match. Terminal bodies are byte-identical; ten pending bodies have
only appended ownership supplements. The structural task's own running log
records its decisions. All 31 archived brief digests match their original bytes.
Swapping two ordered identities and altering an archived byte both make the
corresponding comparison fail. Grove's tree reader accepts the canonical layout.

A review-planning sibling of k140 precedes the Settings branch and k125. Its
independent obligation-preservation review, and any integration it earns, must
finish before product work resumes. No product outcome or closed experiment
budget changes through that prerequisite. The repository documentation check
still reports the two known archived reading-aid links assigned to k23.
