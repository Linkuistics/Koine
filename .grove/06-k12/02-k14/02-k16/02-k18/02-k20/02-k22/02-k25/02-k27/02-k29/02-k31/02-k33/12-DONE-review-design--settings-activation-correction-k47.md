### P2-1. The proposal's finite acceptance limit cannot come from evidence, and the proposal does not say so

The spec records that no evidence-backed failure-rate bound, trial model or
deterministic rule exists for the intermittent failure (spec:90-104). It says a
finite sample cannot falsify an unrestricted intermittent rival. It says a
statistical rejection rule would need explicit agreement as a standard. The
assessment's own candidate row says the closed ABBA sample shows that both
orders can succeed (assessment:71).

The proposal then asks for "explicit agreement on finite acceptance limits"
(spec:216) and "explicit agreement on that acceptance limit" (assessment:144-145).
Neither place states what follows from the spec's own analysis. Any finite bound
for the reversal evaluation is a declared convention, not a rejection rule
derived from evidence. Neither place names the outcome at that bound that would
reject the reversal as a repair.

Consequence: Antony would agree to a limit whose evidential status is undefined.
K46 could then convert "permit design" into "sample until a convention is met".
Smallest correction: the proposal states that the bound is a convention under the
spec's rate-model gap, and names the outcome that rejects the reversal.

### P2-2. The proposal's feasibility evidence is instrumented, and the proposal does not say so

"the historical demonstration that the order can succeed" (spec:206) without it.
The original order also succeeded in A04, so feasibility is symmetric between

Consequence: the one evidential leg of the recommendation reads stronger than
the retained evidence. No uninstrumented reversed-order run exists, and the
reader of the proposal cannot see that. Smallest correction: both proposal
