- Finding 1, medium. KEEPALIVE-CALLER.md lines 63 to 67 define `L` as an owner operation with its own ordinal. They say the existing local family applies and that the specification adds no deadline. The owner spec has two local rows: the successful poll record and the evidence-class failure record, each 0/22 seconds (settings-keepalive-owner.md lines 791 to 792). Unknown operations refuse admission (line 795). Indices 16 to 19, 21 and 24 (lines 143 to 148 and 151) are enumeration, hash and comparison passes. Their inputs are the declared inputs, the candidate bundle, every produced object and the stopped golden config, disk and NVRAM. Neither local row names such work. Under 22 seconds, index 24 is unlikely to complete. The k4 handoff hashed the golden disk image, and a macOS guest image is not 22-second work. Indices 16 and 21 carry the same risk. A required audit then expires on every run, and acceptance is impossible. Lines 66, 67 and 302 put the local children inside the operation. Lines 283 to 287 bound the work before admission. The boundary between the computation and the recorded operation is therefore unclear. State which it is. If the work runs inside the operation, declare a local-audit family whose whole allowance is a k126 input. Note that the accounting requires a scheduling proof and review for a new allowance (KEEPALIVE-ACCOUNTING.md line 237). Post-suppression collection does not touch the 152-second tick argument, and the note can say so. If the work runs before submission, state where its time and processes land in `L_p`, and keep the 22-second record row.

- Finding 2, medium. Lines 72 to 74 say each failed collection item keeps its result, and later applicable items may run once the owner resolves its holder. Line 249 reserves a failure envelope of 25 submissions. The owner spec grants one twenty-second containment window per supervisor role, from the first failure that requires termination. Repeated failures cannot restart it (lines 924 to 934). Collection admission requires the active holder to be settled (line 98). A collection CLI that outlives its whole allowance needs termination. Only the first such failure, or the scenario failure before it, receives the window. A later expiring collection child receives no policy signal, stays unsettled and blocks every later item. So at most one termination-requiring collection failure is resolvable per slot, and none when the scenario failure already spent the window. The artifact states neither limit. An implementer may add a signal outside policy. K126 may budget a serial path of several expired items that is unreachable. Add one sentence after line 74 that states the limit, or propose a declared per-item containment allowance for review.
