### P1-1. The amendment does not say which excluded repair classes it admits

The spec amends "only the requirement that native causal discrimination/disposition
precede repair evaluation" (docs/specs/settings-activation-discrimination.md:99).
The ancestor criteria exclude more than driver waits. The k16 brief says "A
driver wait/retry or a repeated activation/input action is no demonstrated
repair". Every later brief down to k33 repeats that sentence or its short form.
The k12 brief records that menu-tracking deferral is no demonstrated repair.
CAUSAL-PLAN.md:231-233 excludes an alternate API, a yield, async deferral and
repeated activation for the experiment subject.

The spec carries forward only the driver side: "no driver waits/retries/repeated-input
repair" (line 165). It names "a successful alternative API alone" as insufficient
justification (line 93). It does not say whether four candidate classes are
admissible for the behavioural-repair design or remain excluded as workarounds.
The classes are application-side repeated activation, deferral to a later
run-loop pass, a yield-based activation call and an alternative activation API.

The amendment also supplies no test for "justified by public API contracts"
(line 85) once cause is set aside. Apple's contract lets the system decline
activation. ORDER-RESULTS.md:78-87 records that the checked sources prescribe no
sequence that explains or avoids the recorded refusal. So no stated public
contract can justify a candidate on its own, and the spec does not say what would.

Consequence: Antony must decide on a route whose admissibility boundary is
undefined. The k42 leaf inherits the same gap: its Done when preserves "no
driver wait/retry/repeated-input repair" and nothing about application-side
repetition or deferral. Smallest correction: the amendment names the candidate
classes it admits and the classes it keeps excluded. It also states the
justification test a candidate must pass without a cause.

The spec summarises the failure-adjacent contrast as TextEdit requesting

- The success rows 374-375 (`403fb[SetFrontProcessWithInfo]: setFrontmostProcess`

These are private log rows with no established semantics, and a missing

2 against them.

### P2-2. Choice B's revisit condition is stronger than the scope table says

For the modality probe, the spec states that no evidence-backed probability
bound, trial model or deterministic rule exists (lines 59-61). It adds that four
outcomes cannot manufacture one. That reasoning is general. A rival of the form
"failure can occur regardless of X" has no finite falsifier without a
probability bound. Two-sided finite falsification therefore needs either a rate
model or a deterministic manipulation, a condition under which one outcome is
certain.

No retained comparison shows either. The ordering study failed 1 of 2
original-order guests and 0 of 2 reversed. The k4 sample failed 3 of 4. Every
other sample has one or two guests.

Choice B (line 100) says "Revisit only with independent evidence that identifies
a controllable condition and finite falsifiers for both predictions". It offers
a targeted primary-source investigation as the way to find that evidence. It does
not tell Antony that such evidence must include a deterministic manipulation or
a rate model, neither of which the investigation ever saw. Consequence: Antony cannot weigh B against A
without this. B's condition may be unattainable in principle for an intermittent
failure, not merely unmet today. Smallest correction: state the structural
reason in the scope table row for B.

### P3-1. Candidate row 2 overstates the absence of predictions

Row 2 says "Neither a positive causal prediction nor a finite counterprediction
follows from the observed op values" (line 55). The competing-interaction
direction does yield both. Its positive prediction is that failure needs the
competing application's key reclaim. Its finite falsifier is one qualified

What is missing is the rival's finite falsifier, the same one-sided shape as
Hclick against Hshared in CAUSAL-PLAN.md:52-54. The row's other argument stands:
closing or replacing TextEdit is not a matched intervention. The conclusion
survives, the stated reason does not.
