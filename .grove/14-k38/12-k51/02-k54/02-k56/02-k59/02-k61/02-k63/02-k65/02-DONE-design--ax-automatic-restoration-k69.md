# ax-automatic-restoration-k69

## Goal
Exercise a genuine automatic termination/restoration after AX endpoint admission,
or establish the precise platform conflict, preserving strict process lifetime.

## Context
- Read `docs/verification/direct-ax-endpoint.md`, receiver allocation and
  transitions. The frozen exec case passed; restoration did not. Do not treat
  the live-target outcome as a passing lifecycle test.
- The signed/hardened transition holder admitted target PID 905, observed its
  live minimize control, then issued no AX requests while waiting. The target
  closed/hid but stayed live under simulated memory pressure; the holder exited
  25 without a successor. A no-AX control (original fixture, PID 937) terminated
  automatically. This suggests an attachment effect but does not isolate it
  from window style or launch history. All input hashes and actual outputs are
  preserved beside the report.
- `task fixture:ax-transitions` builds the holder; `--hold <pid> <successor-file>`
  awaits a decimal independently observed successor PID for up to 1800 100-ms
  polls. Every native/GUI operation belongs in TestAnyware, host builds only.
- `task fixture:ax-restoration-target` builds the minimizable variant of the
  existing automatic-termination fixture. The earlier variant without that
  style and immediate minimize readback were calibration problems. The current
  holder polls the positive control; do not remove the control to force progress.

## Done when
- Isolate why the admitted target remained live, using matched controls for
  AX access/client lifetime versus other fixture differences. Generic pressure
  invocation or a successful launcher is not evidence of process termination.
- Either execute actual automatic restoration through continuously held admitted
  task/endpoint rights, with independent successor observations, or give the
  original k65/k63 contract a precise evidenced conflict and recommendation.
- Account for any diagnostic client-port lifetime manipulation separately from
  endpoint identity and policy. An invalid client descriptor cannot stand in for
  a dead target endpoint when checking refused effects. No injection, privileged
  helper, target cooperation or public-client private API becomes a product path.
- Preserve inputs, exact completed exit records, native lifecycle and PSN evidence
  (PSN is never the incarnation), update report/ADR/visual discussion, and leave
  top-level routing to k68. No k52 implementation or product agreement.

## Decisions (running log)

- Use the identical minimizable K65AutoTerm build in disposable TestAnyware
  clone `koine-k69-restoration` for no-AX and admitted-holder controls. Observe
  actual process state and TAL lifecycle records; pressure invocation alone is
  not a transition. Keep the original holder's positive minimize control.
- Separate three lifetimes: captured task right, target AX endpoint and local
  AX client port. If client-port retirement enables termination, retain the
  first two continuously and require a valid replacement client descriptor
  with a live-destination control before crediting refused old-endpoint effects.
  This is diagnostic isolation, not an approved product connection policy.
- The clean identical-build no-AX control automatically terminated at PID 847;
  the original admitted holder kept restored PID 869 live, hidden and ineligible
  under pressure. Exclude the first calibration because reopening it unhides it.
- The single adversarial reviewer found two actionable measurement defects:
  unrelated control-window activity between successor observations, and exit zero
  on generic failures. Move the baseline after control teardown and require dead
  task/endpoint rights plus invalid-destination results for every primitive.
  Check the corrected branches in source; their successor-dependent behavior
  remains unexecuted. Native records establish the live-target conflict only.
  No second reviewer or new review stage is needed for this bounded diagnostic.
- A short-lived read-only public-AX witness also leaves the identical target
  hidden but ineligible (PID 994). Minimize/restore is therefore not needed to
  reproduce the observation. Retiring the owned client port alone did not enable
  termination while that original public witness was still part of the setup.
  Preserve the first owned-client instrument as `client-port-probe.m` and remove
  only the original public witness for the direct-only follow-up. Keep audited
  admission and positive minimize/readback/restore; independently witness the
  restored successor after the separate client-validity control.
- The direct-only run also remains live after client-port retirement. One public
  AXRole read, with no NSWorkspace, effects, retained task or long-lived reader,
  reproduces the ineligible state (PID 1065). A second no-AX run automatically
  terminates PID 1096 and restores 1119 with the same PSN/boot. Conclude the
  bounded AX-contact lifecycle conflict; neither source-level policy mechanism
  nor every-app impossibility is established. Keep strict lifetime and withhold
  full binding adoption. `docs/verification/ax-automatic-restoration.md` carries
  evidence, alternatives and reversal conditions for k68/k66/k57.
- The owned-client holders were deliberately stopped with SIGTERM/status 143
  after the live-state observations, not credited as passes or natural timeouts.
  Their replacement-client and successor-effect branches did not execute.
  Preserve these exact outcomes and the original holder's normal exit 25.
